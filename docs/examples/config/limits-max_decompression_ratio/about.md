The most output a decompression may produce per octet of input.

The other half of the same bound, and the half that catches the attack. A ceiling on the output alone
is one a tiny request still reaches, because being small on the wire is what a decompression bomb is
for: the published ones run from ten thousand to one into a billion to one, since they are built from
one repeated octet and a real document is not. So what a decode is actually measured against is the
smaller of the absolute ceiling and this ratio times the octets it was handed.

A call may name a smaller ratio of its own and gets the smaller of the two; nothing raises this one,
and no value switches it off. Written nowhere, it is 1000 to 1, which every corpus a text document
compresses to passes comfortably.

The example prints the ratio in force, refuses a frame under a tighter one while the absolute ceiling
is wide open, and asks to raise the operator's.

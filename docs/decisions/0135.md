# ADR 0135 — A fixed-key shape parameter is one `CoreTy` carrying its arms, and it flattens at the ABI exactly as an options bag does

- **Scope:** how a `Core` member declares a **required, positional, fixed-key shape parameter** —
  `Core\Db::open(Db\Settings $settings, …)` is the first — and how a *discriminated union* of such shapes is
  spelled, checked and passed across the ABI. Does not touch [0036](0036.md) § 3's
  width-subtyped shape *type*, which stays exactly as that ADR wrote it; does not touch
  [0063](0063.md) R2's trailing options bag, which keeps its own registry spelling; does
  not decide **named arguments**, which is `nvs_stdlib::registry`'s own known gap and is a surface question
  about *calls*, not about types. `Db\Settings`'s field list is
  [docs/spec/01-core-library.md](../spec/01-core-library.md) § 18's and is not restated here.
- **Depends on:** [0063](0063.md), whose R2 bag is the mechanism this generalises, and
  [0047](0047.md), whose enum-case types are what separate one arm from another.
- **Validated by:** [crates/nvs-types/tests/core_members.rs](../../crates/nvs-types/tests/core_members.rs) —
  `a_host_on_a_sqlite_settings_literal_is_a_compile_error` and
  `a_sqlite_driver_on_a_server_settings_literal_is_a_compile_error` hold § 2's selection over `Db\Settings`'s
  two arms, by a key one arm lacks and by a value the other arm's `driver` refuses.

> **In short:** a fixed-key shape parameter is **`CoreTy::Shape(&[&[CoreField]])`** — a non-empty list of
> **arms**, each an ordered field list, one arm for the ordinary case and two or more for a discriminated
> union. It is a *whole parameter*, never nested and never a member of `CoreTy::Union`, and it flattens at
> the call site into **one ABI argument per field of the arms merged in order, deduplicated by name** —
> exactly what `CoreTy::Options` already does, so no runtime representation of a shape appears and no helper
> learns a new calling convention. A field carries an optional default: `None` makes it required, `Some(c)`
> makes it omittable and `c` is materialized at the call site, inheriting the bag's own pairing of a
> field's nullability with the constant its omission fills
> ([0147](0147.md) § 1). The arms are **pairwise disjoint**, so exactly one accepts a written literal and the
> "discriminant" is a consequence of that rather than a declared field; `Db\Settings`'s separator is
> `driver`, an `rule:types/literal-types` enum-case-typed field, which is the whole of why a `host` on a SQLite literal is a
> compile error. Both registry spellings intern to **one** checked type, so `E0453` and `E0454` widen from
> "option" to "shape key" and **no new diagnostic code is needed** — a *missing* required key is an ordinary
> argument-type mismatch, unlike an *extra* key, which `rule:types/shape-type`'s width subtyping would otherwise accept.

## Context

`Core\Db::open` is blocked, and not on anything about databases. [0067](0067.md) § 2 writes
`open(Db\Settings $settings, {shared?: bool}): Db\Connection`, and the registry has no way to declare that
first parameter at all. Every shape a `Core` member has ever taken is [0063](0063.md)
R2's **trailing options bag** — `CoreTy::Options`, which is last by construction, unnamed, omittable in
full, and has every one of its fields optional. `Db\Settings` is none of those things: it is positional,
named, required, and half its fields are required too.

The bag's own design already settled the hard half of the question, and `nvs_stdlib::registry`'s module doc
records it: a `Core` shape is **not** an `rule:types/object-top` shape, because `rule:types/shape-type`'s width subtyping accepts a
field the target does not name and a mistyped key must be an error; and a bag **flattens** at the call site
into one ABI argument per declared field, because the alternative builds an `array<mixed>` per call and
needs a spelling the IR does not have. Both hold verbatim for a required shape parameter. What is genuinely
new is three things: a field that may not be omitted, a parameter that is not last, and a **union of two
shapes** — the form § 2 needs so that the SQLite arm can carry a `path` and no `host`.

The second of those is the one that decides the shape of this ADR, because it is where the obvious answer
fails. `CoreTy::Union` already exists and already means "this position admits any of these", so
`Union([Shape(A), Shape(B)])` is the first thing anyone writes. It cannot work: `Ty::Union` is *flattened,
deduplicated and sorted by member `TypeId`*, and the flatten order of a shape's fields is the one thing
`Ty::Options` refuses to sort, because that order **is** the ABI. A union of shapes would have its arms
reordered by the interner and its arguments would land in slots nobody declared.

## Decision

### 1. `CoreTy::Shape` is a list of arms, and one arm is the ordinary case

```rust
pub struct CoreField {
    pub name: &'static str,
    pub ty: CoreTy,
    /// `None` — required. `Some(c)` — omittable, and `c` is what an omitting
    /// call site passes, exactly as a `CoreOption`'s default is.
    pub default: Option<Const>,
}

// in `CoreTy`
Shape(&'static [&'static [CoreField]]),
```

The outer slice is the **arms**; it is never empty. One arm is a plain fixed-key shape parameter; two or
more is a discriminated union. Making the union a property of the one type rather than a composition of two
is what keeps declaration order — the interner never sees a construct it is entitled to canonicalize — and
it is also the only arrangement in which *every* arm is guaranteed to flatten. A `CoreTy::Union` whose arms
were a shape and an `int` would be a type with no ABI at all, and nothing would refuse it.

So the restriction is stated on the other side and held by a test: **a `Shape` is only ever a whole
parameter.** It is never a member of a `Union`, never a `CoreOption`'s or a `CoreField`'s own type, never
inside a `Nullable`, an `Array` or a `Variadic`. `a_shape_is_only_ever_a_whole_parameter` holds it, the way
`a_callback_result_type_is_only_ever_a_whole_parameter` already holds `CallableTo`'s.

Unlike the bag, a shape parameter is an ordinary parameter in every other respect: it appears in
`CoreMethod::params` at its own position with a name in `CoreMethod::names`, and it carries a
`CoreMethod::defaults` entry only if it is itself optional. `CoreTy::Options` keeps its own variant and is
not folded into this one — the two differ in *call-site rules*, not in checking, and the bag's four
properties (last, unnamed, omittable, never in `defaults`) are worth more enforced by a type than restated
in a test.

### 2. The arms are pairwise disjoint, and the discriminant falls out of that

Checking a written literal is "**exactly one arm accepts it**", where an arm accepts when the literal's keys
are exactly the arm's required keys plus any subset of its optional ones, each value assignable to its
field's declared type. Zero arms accepting is the error the call site sees; two accepting is a registry bug,
not a program's, so it is refused **statically**: `a_shapes_arms_are_pairwise_disjoint` proves for every
pair that some field name they both declare has non-overlapping types, or that one arm requires a key the
other does not declare at all.

That is the whole of what [0067](0067.md) § 2 means by *discriminated* union. Nothing declares
`driver` to be a discriminant; it separates the arms because
[0047](0047.md)'s enum-case types make `Driver::Sqlite` and
`Driver::MySql|Driver::MariaDb|Driver::Postgres|Driver::SqlServer` disjoint sets, and disjointness is all
arm selection ever needed. A rule naming a discriminant field would be a second, weaker way of saying the
same thing, and would have nothing to say about a future union separated by a required key instead.

### 3. The ABI is the arms merged in order, deduplicated by name

A shape argument expands at the call site — `nvs_ir::lower::lower_call_args`, the same function that expands
a bag — into one argument per field of the **merged field list**: the arms in declaration order, each arm's
fields in declaration order, a name already emitted skipped. Where a name is declared by more than one arm
it occupies **one** slot whose type is the union of the arms' declarations for it; that is what lets
`Db\Settings`'s `driver` slot arrive as one `Driver` value the helper switches on.

Every slot the written literal does not fill passes a `Const` — the field's own default where it has one,
and `Const::Null` for a field belonging to an arm the caller did not write. The helper therefore reads its
discriminant first and then reads only the slots that arm declares, which the type check has already
guaranteed are filled.

For this to be readable as "filled", the constant standing for *omitted* must be one no written value can
also be, so **a field's nullability and its omission constant are paired**
([0147](0147.md) § 1): a field that admits no
`null` omits as `Const::Null`, and a field that admits one — a nullable, a union with a null arm, or a
`Mixed` — omits as `rule:classes/an-unwritten-property-read-throws`'s never-written marker instead, so `{a: null}` and a missing `a` stay two
arguments. `a_shape_field_is_never_nullable` holds that pairing over every registered row. A nullable
field is admitted only where the member has a removal to offer and a written `null` performs it, which is
0147 § 4's rule rather than this one's.

`Core\Db::open`'s merged list is therefore the server arm's ten fields in spec order, then the SQLite arm's
`path`, then the trailing bag's `shared` — twelve, so the helper is an ordinary `args: [12]`.

A **qualifier classification lands on the field**, not on the parameter: `host` is `Qual::Sink` because
[0067](0067.md) § 3 makes an address one, while the parameter as a whole classifies nothing. The
spec's Q column already writes it that way (`open`'s reads **sink** (host)), so
`every_member_parameter_carries_a_qualifier_classification` walks into a shape's fields rather than gaining
an exemption.

### 4. Two registry spellings, one checked type

`Ty::Options` is generalised to carry a per-field required flag and is **renamed `Ty::CoreShape`**: it is no
longer only a bag, and a variant named for the narrower of its two uses would be wrong in half of them. Both
`CoreTy::Options` and `CoreTy::Shape` intern into it through `nvs_types::core_lib` — a bag as one arm with
every field optional — so the exact-key check, the flatten, and the diagnostics are written once and the
bag's every existing behaviour is the all-optional special case of this one.

`ParamDoc::shape` needs no widening at all: it is already "one entry per key", and for a union it lists the
**merged** key set in ABI order, each `ShapeKeyDoc::desc` saying which arm the key belongs to.
`a_documented_rows_param_docs_agree_with_its_names` extends to cover it.

### 5. No new diagnostic code

`E0453` (`E_OPTIONS_NOT_A_LITERAL`) and `E0454` (`E_UNKNOWN_OPTION`) widen from "options bag" to "shape
key" — one clause each in their doc comments — and cover the two conditions that are not ordinary type
errors: an argument that is not a written shape literal, and a key the member does not declare. A **missing
required key** needs no code of its own, because an incomplete literal genuinely *is* a value of the wrong
type and the existing argument-type mismatch already reports it, naming the parameter. That asymmetry is not
an accident: an *extra* key is the one that needed a code, precisely because `rule:types/shape-type`'s width subtyping
would otherwise have accepted it.

This matters more than it looks. Both type diagnostic bands are full — `E04xx` at `E0499` and its `E07xx`
continuation at `E0799` — so a new type diagnostic would have forced a third band open as a side effect of
adding a parameter kind, which is the wrong reason to make that decision.

## Consequences

- **`Core\Db::open` is unblocked**, and with it every future member taking a fixed-key shape. The
  `nvs_stdlib::db` module doc's known gap `nvs-stdlib/buffered-read-is-not-held-to` and `nvs_stdlib::registry`'s "a matter of named arguments rather
  than shapes" both close on the same slice.
- **A shape argument must be written as a literal at the call site**, so a program cannot build a `Settings`
  in a helper and pass it on. This is the bag's restriction and it is real: § 2's motivating case is
  one-database-per-tenant, where the *fields* are computed and only the braces are written, which this
  allows. Lifting it means giving a shape a runtime representation, which is a much larger decision than
  this one and would be taken for its own reasons.
- **The cost is one ABI slot per field of every arm**, paid on every call including the one that writes the
  smaller arm — twelve arguments for `open`, four of which a SQLite call leaves `null`. That is priority 5
  spent to buy priorities 3 and 4: no allocation on the path, and no shape value for the runtime to own.
  Arms are a member's own declaration and stay small; a member with enough arms for this to matter is a
  member that wanted an enum.
- **`Ty::Options` is renamed**, touching `nvs-types` (the interner, `expr`'s check, `core_lib`),
  `nvs-ir`'s `lower/call.rs` and `nvs-cli`'s `meta.rs`. Mechanical, and it is the whole of the churn.
- **`CoreTy::Union` can never contain a shape**, which is a real narrowing of a type that otherwise composes
  freely. It buys arm order, which is the ABI.

## Alternatives rejected

- **`CoreTy::Union([Shape(A), Shape(B)])` — the union as a pair, reusing the type that exists.** The first
  thing to try and the reason this ADR is not one sentence: `Ty::Union` sorts its members by `TypeId`, and a
  shape's field order is the ABI. Making the interner preserve order for unions whose members happen all to
  be shapes is a special case in the one place canonicalization has to be unconditional to be worth
  anything.
- **A runtime shape value, passed as one argument.** It answers the arity question outright and the
  allocation objection is weak for `open` specifically, which is opening a socket anyway. It fails on the
  other end: no runtime representation of a shape exists — `ExprKind::ObjectLiteral` has no lowering of its
  own at all — so this trades a merged field list in `lower_call_args` for a new value kind in the IR and
  the runtime, to serve one member. Simplicity of the implementation is priority 4 and this spends it badly.
- **Reusing `CoreOption` for a shape's fields, with `default: Option<Const>`.** One struct instead of two,
  at the price of making a bag's "every option has a default, so the bag is optional by construction"
  a *test* rather than a property of the type. Trading a type-enforced invariant for a checked one is the
  wrong direction, and the bag's optionality is load-bearing in `MethodSig::required()`.
- **A declared discriminant field on the type.** `Shape { discriminant: "driver", arms: … }` reads well and
  matches how § 2 describes `Db\Settings`. It adds nothing checking does not already do, and it cannot
  describe a union separated by a required key rather than by a case — so it would be a second rule to keep
  in agreement with the first.
- **Folding `CoreTy::Options` into `CoreTy::Shape`.** One registry spelling is tempting, but the bag's
  trailing-ness, namelessness and omittability would become flags on a general type, checked by tests
  instead of held by the enum. The two are one *type* and two *call-site rules*, and § 4 splits them on
  exactly that line.

## Verification

- `a_shape_is_only_ever_a_whole_parameter`, `a_shapes_arms_are_pairwise_disjoint` and
  `a_shape_field_is_never_nullable` — registry tests over `CLASSES`, in `nvs-stdlib`, beside the ones that
  already hold `CoreTy::Options`, `CoreTy::Union` and `CoreTy::CallableTo` to their own restrictions.
- `a_documented_rows_param_docs_agree_with_its_names` extended: a shape parameter's `ParamDoc::shape` is the
  merged key list in ABI order.
- A `.nvst` case per condition at `Core\Db::open`: a server-arm literal and a SQLite-arm literal both
  compile; a `host` on a SQLite literal is `E0454`; a literal missing a required key is the argument-type
  mismatch; a variable written where the literal belongs is `E0453`.
- `a_db_open_target_in_a_denied_range_fails` — the goal's own open check, which is what having the type at
  all is for: `open`'s `host` is program-supplied, so [0058](0058.md)'s denied
  ranges apply to it in full where a `connect`-named endpoint is pre-approved ([0067](0067.md) § 3).

# ADR 0162 — `Core\Net` is one socket API over three transports, and the grant follows the direction rather than the transport

- **Scope:** what a program may open through `Core\Net`, which capability each of those openings asks,
  and why every one of them parks on the runtime's reactor. It does not decide the member spellings —
  those are the registry rows the stages after it write — and it decides nothing about TLS, which
  `Core\Crypto` owns and which a `Core\Net` socket does not acquire after the fact.
- **Depends on:** [0051](0051.md), which placed `Core\Net` in the Native tier over the runtime's own
  reactor; [0058](0058.md), whose address policy governs every program-supplied destination;
  [0142](0142.md) § 6, which named `net.local` and left it off the roster for this record to put on it;
  [0052](0052.md), whose closed doors are what the surface below may not reopen.
- **Validated by:** the conformance cases the four rules below name once the stages after this one land
  them; nothing holds this record on its own, which is why the rules it creates are `designed`.

> **In short:** `Core\Net` is one class over TCP, UDP and Unix-domain sockets, and a program reaches it
> through five entry points rather than through PHP's three overlapping APIs. Which capability an
> opening asks is decided by **what the program is doing** and not by which transport it is doing it
> over: reaching outward is `net.connect` and carries [0058](0058.md)'s address policy, binding is
> `net.listen` and carries none, and a socket path at either end is `net.local`. Every socket parks on
> the runtime's existing reactor, closes with the request that opened it, and gains no reliability
> layer it did not have on the wire.

## Context

### 1. Three PHP APIs for one job, and the shape none of them has

PHP reaches a socket through `socket_*`, through `stream_socket_*` and through `fsockopen`, and the
three differ in what they return, in how a timeout is spelled, in whether a failure is `false` or a
warning, and in whether the result is a stream the rest of the language understands.
[0051](0051.md) placed all three behind one `Core\Net` for that reason, and
`docs/spec/02-php-migration.md` already routes every surviving row to it.

What the migration table settled is the *count*. What it left open is the part that matters for
security: PHP's socket APIs make **one call** do connect-or-bind depending on an argument's spelling —
`stream_socket_client("unix:///run/x.sock")` and `stream_socket_client("tcp://example.com:80")` are the
same function, distinguished by a scheme prefix. `rule:security/a-path-is-not-a-url` forbids exactly
that dispatch, so the surface cannot be a transliteration however small the member count is.

### 2. The capability roster answers a host, and has nothing to say about a bind or a path

`crates/nvs-config/src/capability.rs` knows one network grant: `net.connect`, asked at `Scope::Host`
and carrying [0058](0058.md)'s denied-range table. Every other outbound door in the tree — `Core\Http`,
`Core\Db::open`, `Core\Mail` — is a caller of it. Nothing in the roster answers "may this program bind
a port", because until now nothing in the language could ask: the server's own listening socket is the
host's, bound before any program runs, and `rule:concurrency/a-connection-is-a-root-isolate`'s
`Core\Socket` upgrades a connection the server already accepted rather than opening one.

`net.local` was named by [0142](0142.md) § 6 and deliberately left off the roster, on the ground that a
capability with no caller is a row that cannot be exercised. This record is the caller arriving.

## Investigation

### 3. The parking contract already exists, and a second event loop would have to be worse than it

`crates/nvs-host/src/net.rs` is a `Read` and a `Write` that hand the core back instead of blocking it:
`NvsStream<S>` over a `Source`, `NvsListener` accepting on the same four functions, `NvsUnix` at the
same contract with a local address, and a `Deadline` trait forwarding a bound down a wrapper stack so
that only the innermost stream waits. `crates/nvs-host/src/reactor.rs` is the readiness half — `mio`
underneath, a `TaskId` on top.

Three of the four transports `Core\Net` needs are therefore already written and already carrying the
server's own traffic. The exception is UDP: that crate has no datagram type at all, because nothing
has needed one. Adding `NvsUdp` beside `NvsTcp` is an addition *inside* the contract rather than a
second mechanism beside it.

An extra event loop is not a smaller change than that. It would be a second thread-and-poll structure
whose fairness, shutdown, deadline and drain semantics all have to be made to agree with the reactor's
by hand, and whose disagreements only appear under load. `rule:concurrency/one-scheduler` already
refuses a second scheduler for the same reason, and the standing form of this constraint is that a
shape which cannot be expressed over the reactor is cut rather than granted its own loop.

### 4. What a bind is dangerous for is not what the address policy protects against

[0058](0058.md) § 3's table denies loopback, the private ranges, link-local and the unspecified
address, and it denies them because a *program-supplied outbound* target reaching those ranges is the
request-forgery case: the program persuades the runtime to speak to something only the runtime can
reach. Every clause of that reasoning is about an address the program reaches **outward** to.

A bind inverts every term. Binding `127.0.0.1` is the *contained* case and binding `0.0.0.0` is the
exposed one, so applying the same table to a listener would deny the safe spelling and permit the
dangerous one. What a bind actually risks is different in kind: occupying a port another service
expects, or exposing a surface to a network the operator did not intend. That is answered by naming
the endpoints rather than by classifying their ranges.

## Options considered

### 5. One `net.connect` widened, or one grant per direction

Widening `net.connect` to cover binds and socket paths keeps the roster at its current size and gives
one key an operator has to reason about. It fails on the same ground [0142](0142.md) § 6 refused it
for paths, doubled: entries of three kinds under one name — hosts governed by an address table, bind
endpoints governed by nothing, paths governed by nothing — is one name covering three guarantees, and
an operator who grants an outbound host has silently granted a listener. § 4's inversion makes it
worse than a merely imprecise grant, because the table it carries would be actively wrong for two of
the three kinds.

Three grants is three keys, and the cost is real: an operator writes more, and a reviewer reads more.
That cost buys the property the roster exists for — a grant that says one thing, so that reading it
tells you what the program can do.

### 6. Whether UDP's grant is asked at the bind or at the send

A datagram socket is bound locally and then sends to wherever it is told, so the two obvious readings
are that the socket is the thing granted — one check, at the bind — or that the destination is, which
is a check per `send`.

Granting at the bind alone is what makes UDP the hole in this record. A program with `net.listen` for
its own port could then send a datagram to any address in any denied range, and the whole of
[0058](0058.md) would be reachable by asking for it over the other transport. So the direction, not
the socket, is what the grant follows, and § 8 states it as the general rule rather than as a
patch for UDP.

## Decision

### 7. One class, three transports, five ways in

`Core\Net` opens a socket through five entry points, and each of them says in its name what it does:
an outbound TCP connection, a listening TCP socket, a bound UDP socket, an outbound Unix-domain
connection and a listening Unix-domain socket. Accepting is a member on the listener the fourth and
fifth hand back, not a sixth entry point.

**No entry point decides between transports by reading its argument.** A host and a socket path are
separate members taking separately-typed arguments, so `rule:security/a-path-is-not-a-url`'s refusal —
no member dispatches on the textual content of a path — holds here by construction rather than by a
check. This is the whole of why the surface is five members rather than the two PHP has: the second
member *is* the security property.

The connected transports answer a `Read` and a `Write` shaped like every other stream in the language.
UDP does not, because a datagram socket has no stream to read: it sends and receives whole messages,
addressed one at a time.

### 8. The grant follows the direction, not the transport

Every opening and every send asks exactly one of three grants, chosen by what the program is doing:

| What the program does | Grant | Scope | Address policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reaches a host — a TCP connect, or a UDP `send` to a destination | `net.connect` | `Scope::Host` | yes, on the resolved address |
| Binds an endpoint — a TCP listener, or a UDP socket's local port | `net.listen` | the endpoint, matched exactly | no |
| Opens or binds a socket path | `net.local` | `Scope::Path` | no — there is no address |

A UDP socket therefore asks `net.listen` once for its local port and `net.connect` for **every
destination it sends to**, resolved and checked at the send exactly as a TCP connect is. That is what
§ 6 settled, and it is what makes "the grant follows the direction" a rule rather than a slogan: one
socket can need two grants, because it does two things.

None of the three widens either of the others. A program that may reach `metrics.internal` may not
bind a port; a program that may open `/run/x.sock` may not reach the network; a program that may
listen has been granted nothing outbound.

### 9. `net.listen` names endpoints and carries no address policy

An entry is an `address:port` literal, matched exactly, and `true` is "any endpoint this process may
bind". § 4 is why there is no denied-range table here: the table's terms invert under a bind, and
importing them would deny loopback while permitting the wildcard address.

Exact matching, rather than a pattern or a port range, is the same choice [0058](0058.md) made for
`net.internal` and for the same reason — an operator writing a range hands back more than the one
endpoint they meant, and the value of the grant is that a reviewer can see what a deployment opened.
An entry that does not parse as an endpoint matches nothing, which is `Capabilities::address_refused`'s
existing shape for an unparseable exception.

### 10. `net.local` joins the roster, and it governs both ends of a path

[0142](0142.md) § 6's shape is adopted unchanged — `Scope::Path`, entries that are absolute paths or
directory prefixes, canonicalise-then-prefix under
`rule:security/path-scope-canonicalise-then-prefix`, and no address policy because there is no address
— with one thing that record left open now settled: it governs **binding** a socket path as well as
connecting to one. A program that may create a socket at a path is a program that can be spoken to by
whatever else on the host finds it, and a path the operator did not name is one they cannot have
intended, so both ends ask the same grant.

`rule:config/a-unix-socket-is-admitted-only-where-an-operator-wrote-it` is untouched by this and stays
exactly as true: `Core\Net::connect` still may not take a path, because it is not the member that
takes one.

### 11. UDP is datagrams, and nothing above them

A `Core\Net` datagram socket sends and receives messages, reports what it sent and what arrived, and
offers nothing resembling ordering, retransmission, fragmentation or an acknowledgement. A program
that needs those needs TCP, or needs to build them itself and own the choice.

The refusal is worth writing down because the pressure to add "just a retry" is constant and the
result is always the same: a reliability layer nobody specified, whose failure modes are the
library's while its timing budget is the program's. `rule:core-api/five-placements` puts a real
reliable-datagram protocol where it belongs, which is not Tier 0.

### 12. A socket closes with the request that opened it

There is no persistent socket, no connection pooled across requests and no handle a later request can
find. `rule:security/no-cross-request-state` decides this already; `pfsockopen`'s row in the migration
table records the same thing from PHP's side. A socket's reactor registration is released with the
request's arena, so the process holds one registration per socket in flight and nothing per socket
served.

### 13. `stream_socket_pair` has no answer, so its row becomes `dropped`

A connected pair of anonymous sockets exists to be handed to a forked child. `pcntl` is dropped
([0051](0051.md)), and the one process boundary Novis has is an isolate, which shares nothing
(`rule:security/isolate-shares-nothing`) and so cannot receive a descriptor. Spelling a member for it
would be a member with no caller, which the standing form of this goal refuses: a PHP function with no
Novis answer is a `dropped` row, never a member spelled to fill a hole.

## Diagnostics

A refusal names the grant that was missing and the thing that was asked for, which is
`rule:security/capability-check-at-the-door`'s existing shape and needs no new code: a `net.listen`
refusal reads as the endpoint, a `net.local` refusal as the canonical path, and a `net.connect`
refusal keeps [0058](0058.md)'s two-part answer — the host was not granted, or the address it resolved
to is in a denied range. A UDP `send` refused at its destination is the second of those, at the send
rather than at the open, and says so.

Two new configuration keys mean two new ways to write nonsense in `nvs.toml` — an unparseable
`net.listen` endpoint and a relative `net.local` path — and both are boot-time refusals in the
`E06xx` band rather than run-time surprises, because a grant that silently matches nothing is a
deployment that looks configured and is not.

## Consequences

- **The roster grows by two rows**, and `rule:security/capability-roster-is-closed` still holds: the
  roster is closed to *userland*, and a row is added by a decision record naming its caller, which is
  what this is.
- **What this spends**, per `rule:programs/memory-priority`: one reactor registration per open socket,
  attributable to the request that opened it and released with its arena. Nothing per process, and
  nothing that grows with sockets served.
- **An operator granting network access now writes more than one key.** A deployment that previously
  wrote `net.connect` and was done now writes `net.listen` too if its program binds. That is the
  intended cost of § 5 and the reason the refusal names which key is missing.
- **UDP's per-send check is a resolution on a hot path.** It is the same resolution and the same pin
  a TCP connect already pays, so a program sending many datagrams to one destination pays it many
  times; whether that pin is cached for the socket's life is an implementation question the stages
  after this one answer, and caching it changes no rule here because the destination is re-checked
  whenever it changes.
- **`config/net-local-is-named-and-not-on-the-roster`'s slug outlives its second clause.** The rule
  now states a grant that *is* on the roster; the id is a path frozen by [0142](0142.md) and is not a
  restatement of the title.

## Alternatives rejected

- **A `stream`-shaped surface where one member returns any transport.** It is PHP's shape, and it is
  refused by `rule:security/a-path-is-not-a-url` at the point where the argument's text decides what
  gets opened.
- **A second event loop for sockets a program opens.** § 3: two poll structures that must be made to
  agree, and disagree only under load.
- **`net.connect` widened to cover binds and paths.** § 5: one name covering three guarantees, with
  an address table that is wrong for two of them.
- **Granting UDP once at the bind.** § 6: it makes the other transport a way around [0058](0058.md).
- **A denied-range table for `net.listen`, inverted so that public addresses are the denied set.** It
  reads as symmetry and is a second policy to keep in step with the first, for a risk — exposing a
  port — that naming the endpoint already answers exactly.

## Revisiting

The per-send check in § 8 is the one part with a measurement behind it that has not been taken. If a
datagram-heavy program shows the resolution dominating its send path, the answer is a pin cached on
the socket for as long as the destination is unchanged — not a grant asked once at the bind, which is
the shape § 6 refused. Nothing else here reopens on a measurement.

## Verification

The four rules this record creates are `designed` until the stages after it land them, and each
carries its guard once it does: that a bind is refused without `net.listen` while `net.connect` is
granted, and the converse; that a socket path asks `net.local` at both ends; that a UDP `send` to a
denied address is refused at the send with `net.listen` held; and that a socket opened by one request
is not reachable from the next.

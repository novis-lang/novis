# ADR 0272 — a feature's perf proof is one measured figure and its growth, judged by the doubling ramp on the counts, with callgrind only to find a threshold

- **Scope:** what the **Perf** proof of `rule:testing/feature-proofs` owes: the ramp over the
  feature's bench, a required `// bench: complexity` declaration, and when valgrind may run. Which
  change reruns a bench, and when a figure is current, stay `rule:testing/member-perf-ledger`'s.
- **Depends on:** [0134](0134.md), [0191](0191.md).
- **Validated by:** `tools/nv/test/proof-growth.test.ts`, among them the guard that the plan
  `nv verify` runs never starts valgrind over a bench, and `bun nv proofs --record-perf` over the
  whole bench tree.

> **In short:** a feature's perf proof was one figure, and growth was checked only for a bench that
> chose to declare `// bench: complexity`, by one wall-clock ratio against a `.scale.nvs` sibling with
> a tolerance of three. The proof now runs the doubling ramp of `bun nv scaling` over the feature's
> bench: the per-operation counts must not grow as the batches double, and where the work depends on
> input size, the cost may grow no faster than the bench's declared complexity. Every bench declares
> one, and `quadratic` is never accepted. The counts decide and the clock reports. Valgrind's
> callgrind runs only when the ramp shows no clear growth, only to find the batch at which it is
> clear, and never in `nv verify` or an ordinary bench run.

## Context

### 1. Growth was optional and judged by the clock

[0191](0191.md) let a bench declare `// bench: complexity constant` or `linear` beside a
`<name>.scale.nvs` sibling, and `tools/nv/proofs/perf.ts` compared the two per-operation clock
figures from one sweep against three times the predicted ratio. A bench without the line was never
checked, so a new feature could ship a quadratic member by leaving it out. The check was one ratio
of two clock figures, an upper bound loose enough to miss a slope of 1.5: at a scale factor of 4 a
linear bench may cost 12 times as much per operation, and `n^1.5` costs 8.

### 2. A better instrument already exists

Goal `performance-pass` built `bun nv scaling` (`tools/nv/cmd/scaling.ts`), which runs a bench at
doubling batches and judges the slope of each count's increment, so start-up and set-up cancel out
exactly and the verdict is the same on every machine. It was proven over the whole bench tree, and
every bench that grew too fast was fixed. That tree passing is the precondition for making growth a
proof: switched on any earlier, it would have made a roster of features owe a proof at once.

## Decision

### 3. Perf is one figure and its growth

The **Perf** proof is one measured figure *and its growth*. The figure is the ledger record
`rule:testing/member-perf-ledger` describes, unchanged. The growth is the ramp of
`tools/nv/cmd/scaling.ts` over the same bench, and that tool's module doc is the one home of the
ramp, the agreement test, the ceiling and the bounds. `bun nv proofs --record-perf` and `bun nv
proofs --id '<feature>'` run it with no extra command, and a bench that grows past its bound is a
failing proof under `rule:testing/a-failing-proof-is-fixed-or-recorded`: no record is written.

### 4. Every bench declares its complexity

`// bench: complexity` is required. It is `constant`, `linear`, `nlogn`, or a named class an
algorithm has, and never `quadratic`. `constant` needs only the iteration ramp. Any other class also
needs a size ramp, from the `.scale.nvs` sibling, which takes `start` and `max` as a ladder does in
place of its single `// bench: scale K`. A bench with no declaration fails its proof, so the size
check cannot be skipped by omission. `benches/members/README.md` owns how the line is written.

### 5. The counts decide, and callgrind finds a threshold

The ramp decides on the four counts, which are the same on every run. The clock is read and
reported and never fails a proof, sets an iteration count or decides that a bench is proven. Where
the counts stay flat while the work happens inside a Rust member, and the clock is too noisy to read
before the ramp reaches its ceiling, the proof runs the same batches under `valgrind
--tool=callgrind`, whose instruction count is as exact as a count. It does so only to find the batch
at which the growth is clear. The bench's `// bench: iterations N` is set from that batch, with a
note beside it that callgrind measured it, and later runs use N with the counts and the clock and do
not start valgrind again until the bench's definition changes or the ramp at N is unclear again. A
bench still unclear after callgrind is reported, not judged.

### 6. Valgrind never runs in `nv verify`

`nv verify` and an ordinary bench run never start valgrind. Callgrind costs minutes per bench and
needs WSL on Windows; a gate that could start it would make every verify as slow as its slowest
unclear bench. `tools/nv/test/proof-growth.test.ts` fails if the plan `nv verify` runs could.

## Consequences

- The `COMPLEXITY_RE` and `SCALE_TOLERANCE` check in `tools/nv/proofs/perf.ts` is replaced by a
  call to the ramp, and the `scale_ratio` figure stops being the growth verdict.
- Every bench under `benches/members/` that lacks `// bench: complexity` gains one, from what goal
  `performance-pass`'s ramp measured.
- [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) rule 9 and [conventions.md](../agent/conventions.md) § *Feature
  proofs* name the bench with its growth.
- A recording sweep costs more per bench than one timed run, because the ramp runs several batches.
  It is paid when a figure is re-measured, not on every verify.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Keep the one-ratio check and make the line required.** It would close the omission and keep a
  clock verdict that a noisy machine can pass or fail at random, and the tolerance § 1 shows
  missing a slope of 1.5.
- **Run callgrind on every bench.** Its verdict is exact, and it costs minutes per bench on every
  recording, for a question the counts already answer for most benches.

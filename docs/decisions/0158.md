# ADR 0158 — security in an editor is the editor's chapter, and the security chapter is the running language's

- **Scope:** where a rule about concealing a `secret` on screen lives, and the test that decides it. The
  rules themselves are unchanged in substance; what changes is which chapter owns them, and therefore
  what a reader takes them to promise.
- **Depends on:** [0101](0101.md) — the concealment, the reveal and the four rules this re-homes;
  [0004](0004.md) — the priority ordering that makes "security" the word this record is protecting.

> **In short:** five rules under `security/` were about what VS Code draws over a buffer, and a reader
> meeting `security/reveal-is-explicit-and-window-local` in the ground rules had no way to tell that
> from a guarantee the compiler enforces. They move to `ide/`, and four rules already in `ide/` — the
> stdio framing, the version refusal, the dependency allowlist and PhpStorm's absent redaction — join
> them as one contiguous *Security in the editor* section at the end of that chapter. **The test is
> whether the rule still holds with no editor running.** `security/` keeps every rule that does,
> including the two that look editor-shaped and are not: the AST dump's placeholder is emitted by the
> compiler into the JSON, and the test runner's redacted diff is the runner's. Nothing is weakened and
> no mechanism changes — a rule's id is its address, and five addresses moved.

## Context

`docs/rules/security.md` is the chapter behind priority 1 of the ordering, the one thing
[0004](0004.md) says is never traded. That gives every rule filed there a specific weight: a reader
takes it as something the language will not let them get wrong.

Five of its rules were not that. `security/redaction-ranges-come-from-the-server`,
`security/redaction-covers-bytes-only`, `security/redaction-does-not-reach`,
`security/reveal-is-explicit-and-window-local` and `security/tainted-has-no-default-decoration` are
about a decoration an editor draws — window-local, cosmetic by their own admission, and absent
entirely when the file is opened by anything but the reference client. Read in `ground-rules.md`,
where each rule is one line and its chapter is the only context, "a reveal is per range, window-local,
and does not survive the editor closing" reads as a statement about Novis. It is a statement about VS
Code.

The confusion is not hypothetical and it runs in the dangerous direction: it invites a reader to count
a rendering as part of the guarantee, which is the mistake `ide/redaction-does-not-reach` exists to
prevent. A chapter that mixes the two undoes that rule by filing it.

## Decision

### 1. The test is whether the rule survives with no editor running

A rule belongs to `security/` when it holds for a program the compiler has accepted, whether that
program is serving a request from a container with no terminal attached or being run by `nvs run`. It
belongs to `ide/` when it describes what a client draws, what a server answers over a wire, or what an
extension is allowed to contain.

This is a question with one answer per rule and it needs no judgement: turn the editor off and ask
whether the sentence is still true. Every sink refusing a `secret` survives that. A blur over a string
literal does not.

### 2. Five rules move, and four already-editor rules join them

The five above become `ide/redaction-ranges-come-from-the-server`,
`ide/redaction-covers-bytes-only`, `ide/redaction-does-not-reach`,
`ide/reveal-is-explicit-and-window-local` and `ide/tainted-has-no-default-decoration`.

They are gathered with four rules the `ide` chapter already held and had scattered through three
sections — `ide/stdout-belongs-to-the-protocol` (a stray write corrupts the framing),
`ide/the-extension-refuses-a-binary-it-does-not-understand` (which `nvs` the client will run),
`ide/dependencies-are-allowlisted` (what the client may contain) and
`ide/phpstorm-draws-no-redaction-at-m4b` — into one contiguous **Security in the editor** section at
the end of the chapter, ordered from the process boundary outward to what is drawn on the glass.

Gathering them is the half of this record that makes the split answerable rather than merely tidy:
"what does security mean when an LSP serves a file to an editor" now has one place to read, and it is
not the place where "what does security mean for a running program" is answered.

### 3. Two rules that look editor-shaped stay in `security/`

`security/redaction-reaches-the-tools-own-renderings` is about the AST dump and the diagnostic
record's renderings. The placeholder is emitted by the compiler, into the JSON, for every caller —
`nvs ast` at a terminal with no editor within reach gets it. Its prose is rewritten here to lead with
that rather than with the editor it used to lean on, and its title changes to say so.

`security/secret-in-a-test-report` is the test runner's redacted diff, which no flag lifts. Same test,
same answer: it holds with the editor off.

Both now cross-reference the editor chapter rather than reading as part of it, and
`ide/redaction-ranges-come-from-the-server` gains the paragraph pointing the other way — that it is a
rendering, that `security/secret-sinks-refuse` is the guarantee, and that an editor drawing nothing is
not running a less safe language.

## Consequences

A rule's id is its address, so five addresses change and every citation of them follows: 76 of them,
across `crates/nvs-lsp`, `crates/nvs-types`, `editors/vscode`, the goal manifests and the records.
Records are frozen in their reasoning, not in their addresses — a `changes:` id that does not resolve
is a finding in `tools/records.py --check` — so the `changes:` blocks and cited ids in
[0016](0016.md), [0033](0033.md), [0040](0040.md), [0088](0088.md), [0099](0099.md),
[0101](0101.md) and [0108](0108.md) are re-addressed without a word of their prose changing.

The published rulebook loses its `security/redaction` page and gains `ide/security-in-the-editor`; the
two remaining rules fold into `security/secrets`, whose blurb now names them.

The `ide` chapter grows to 67 rules and becomes the longest in the book, which is the price of it
being the only place the editor is described. The alternative prices the word *security* instead.

## Alternatives rejected

**A third chapter, `editor-security`.** It reads cleanly in the index and splits the editor across two
homes: a reader of `ide` would have to know that a second chapter holds the half about what the
extension may contain, and every rule in each would need a pointer to the other. The `ide` chapter is
already the one home for the editor, and a section inside it is what "one home per fact" asks for.

**Leaving the ids and re-cutting the published site only.** The site is generated from the rulebook,
so this would have made the rendered chapter honest while `ground-rules.md`, every `rule:` citation in
`crates/` and every id a future session greps for kept saying `security/`. The address is what the
repository actually reads; changing anything else first is changing the label on the wrong thing.

## Verification

`--citations` for the 76 re-addressed tokens, and `python tools/records.py --check` for the `changes:`

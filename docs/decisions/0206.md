# ADR 0206 — Whatever is idle closes the moment it sees the drain, and only work in progress is given a period

- **Scope:** what the drain does with a WebSocket whose program is waiting for its peer and with an
  event stream that has nothing to write, and the seam that lets a read already parked see the
  drain at all. What the drain does with an HTTP connection is [0205](0205.md)'s; what it does with
  a request in flight is [0186](0186.md) § 3's.
- **Depends on:** [0186](0186.md), [0205](0205.md).
- **Validated by:** `crates/nvs-server/src/serve.rs`'s
  `a_websocket_parked_on_its_peer_is_closed_the_moment_the_drain_begins` and
  `a_drain_ends_the_body_cleanly_rather_than_resetting_the_connection`.

> **In short:** a stopping server has no reason to keep anything idle. A WebSocket program parked
> in `receive` was not told of the drain at all until its idle wait ran out, five minutes with
> nothing configured, and then it was given a period on top; an event stream with nothing to write
> was given the period too. Both now close on the poll that finds them idle. What the period bounds
> is work in progress: a request that is moving, and an event stream that is still writing. A
> WebSocket program between one `receive` and the next finishes its step — its `send` completes —
> and its next `receive` ends the connection.

## Context

### 1. A silent WebSocket held a stop for its whole idle wait

`nvs_server::socket`'s `receive` read the drain bit before each wait and set a closing deadline of
"now plus the connection block's drain period" when it saw it. A read already parked when the drain
began never came back to that line: a parked read ends on its deadline, on readiness or on a
cancellation, and a plain wake only sends it round its retry loop to park again on the same
deadline. The module said so in its own words and named the gap as one slice for both readers. The
HTTP reader's half landed in [0205](0205.md); this is the other half. With nothing configured the
idle wait is five minutes, and the accept loop's tail waits for every connection it spawned, so
one browser tab holding a quiet WebSocket held Ctrl-C for five minutes.

### 2. A period is for finishing something

[0186](0186.md) § 3 and `nvs_server::bounds` reasoned that a drain period buys "the frame already on
the wire and the answer to it". A program parked in `receive` has no frame on the wire and nothing
to answer, and an event stream whose cell is empty has nothing to write. The period bought both of
them nothing and cost a stop the whole of it. Every client on both doors already handles the close:
a WebSocket peer reads RFC 6455's `going away` and reconnects, and an `EventSource` comes back after
the wait its `retry:` line gave it.

## Decision

### 3. A parked read is cut by the drain

`nvs_host::NvsStream::cut_at_drain` registers with the server's drain a wake that raises a flag on
the stream and wakes the task parked on it. The stream reads the flag as its readable wait resumes,
and where it is raised the `read` returns `WouldBlock` in place of parking again. That is the answer
a non-blocking codec already returns untouched, so `tungstenite` hands it up with its framing state
intact and `receive` reads the drain bit at the top of its loop. Only the readable wait is cut, and
the flag is lowered by the wait that reads it: a `send` in flight completes on its own clock, and the
close frame written after the cut goes out whole. `receive` registers the cut on its first call,
which is the task the program runs on — the connection task that built the socket is not the one
that parks in it.

### 4. A `receive` that sees the drain closes at once

`receive` closes the connection with `Closing::ShuttingDown` on the first pass that finds the drain
begun — before a wait, after the cut, or on a wake that found nothing to read — and answers `null`
so the program's loop ends as it does on an ordinary disconnect. The connection block's drain period
is no longer an instant in the WebSocket's wait, and `Closing::ShuttingDown` is no longer an answer
of its expiry: the only clocks left on that door are the idle window and the lifetime.

### 5. An event stream with nothing to write ends on the poll that finds it idle

The body's poll that finds the cell empty under a begun drain ends the stream instead of writing a
keep-alive or parking. A stream whose cell holds an event when the drain begins writes it, and one
that keeps writing ends at the period's end as before: the period is the event already in the cell
and the one behind it, which is what it was sized for.

## Consequences

- A stop is bounded by the requests in flight and the event streams still writing, and by nothing
  that is waiting on a peer or has nothing to say.
- A WebSocket program mid-step is unaffected until its next `receive`: its `send` completes, and the
  close comes where it was always going to be reported.
- A WebSocket program that only sends and never calls `receive` still runs to its lifetime, its send
  timeout or its peer's close; nothing here reaches it, and that is unchanged.
- What it spends, per `rule:programs/memory-priority`: one `Arc<AtomicBool>` and one drain
  registration per WebSocket connection, and one atomic swap per readable wait on a stream that
  registered a cut. Nothing on a stream that did not.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Cancel the connection isolate from the accept loop.** The reset the rule exists to prevent, and
  the reason the close was the connection's own in the first place.
- **A wake alone, with `receive` re-reading the bit on each retry.** The retry loop is inside the
  host's `read`, under the codec, and neither can be told to look at a bit: the wake is a hint by
  the parking contract, and honouring it as more than one would break every other wait.
- **Poll the drain on a short deadline.** A parked read woken every few hundred milliseconds to ask
  a question that is almost never yes, on every WebSocket the process holds.

## Verification

- `crates/nvs-server/src/serve.rs`'s `a_websocket_parked_on_its_peer_is_closed_the_moment_the_drain_begins`:
  the program says it is about to wait, the client then ends the loop, and the `going away` close
  arrives within seconds against a five-minute idle bound.
- `crates/nvs-server/src/serve.rs`'s `reload_and_shutdown_close_every_connection_after_the_drain`:
  a drain begun before the first `receive` is read before the wait.
- `crates/nvs-server/src/serve.rs`'s `a_drain_ends_the_body_cleanly_rather_than_resetting_the_connection`
  and `a_drained_stream_still_writing_ends_at_the_periods_end`: an event in the cell is written, an
  empty cell ends the stream at once, and a stream that keeps writing ends at the period's end.

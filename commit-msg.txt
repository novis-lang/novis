feat: enums are integers, and `as` lowers its free and total rows

ADR 0010 makes an enum a closed, named *integer* type whose cases are
compile-time constants. Until now `Ty::Enum` erased to `Ty::Object` in the IR
and no enum reached codegen at all — `examples/enums.mwl` panicked in
`mwl_ir::lower` on its first `as`.

`mwl_types::enums` is the new home for what a declaration says: the backing
type (ADR 0010 § 2) and every case's value, including § 1's C# auto-increment
and the explicit-value-resets-the-counter rule. The backing type travels in
`Ty::Enum(QName, EnumBacking)` rather than a side lookup, because it is part
of what the type is — that is what lets `mwl-ir` lower an enum-typed binding
without re-resolving the declaration. A case's value reaches `mwl-ir` through
`ExprInfo::EnumCase`, so `Rank::Gold` lowers to a plain `ConstInt 2`: no
storage, no descriptor, no allocation, exactly ADR 0010 § 3.

`mwl-ir` gains `Ty::Enum(EnumRepr)` rather than erasing an enum to the integer
under it, for one reason: ADR 0035 § 4 makes an enum case *always* truthy,
where a plain `int` `0` is falsy. Erasing here would silently get
`if (Rank::Bronze)` wrong with nothing downstream able to tell the difference.

`as` itself (ADR 0007 § 2) lowers its free and total rows —
`lower::Lowering::convert` owns the table. Free: same representation, and
ADR 0010 § 5's enum-to-backing row, which is the new `InstKind::Reinterpret`
(a relabelling `mwl-codegen` emits no machine instruction for). Total: a
scalar to `string` reuses the `Helper` conversions `.` already goes through,
and anything to `bool` reuses ADR 0035's truthy table, since `as bool` is the
explicit spelling of exactly that test. Every *checked* row panics naming
itself; each needs a throwing runtime helper, and the integer-into-an-enum row
also needs the case set carried to the check.

`ExprTypeTable` also gains `declared_ty` — the resolved type of an annotation,
recorded at `lower_type`. `mwl_ir::lower::lower_decl_type` reads a declared
type off the AST, which works while every atom is its own answer; an enum name
is the first atom whose meaning needs the symbol table, which `mwl-ir` does
not have.
